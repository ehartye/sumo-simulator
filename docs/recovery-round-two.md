# Ten more recovery experiments (14–23)

This round compares every candidate against fixed full-duty (03) and close-memory (01): three sensor-noise seeds, both sides, 120 seconds, twelve bouts per candidate. Potential promotion requires pooled score >=75%, neither opponent below 50%, no faults, and inspected useful recovery evidence. These remain uncalibrated simulator comparisons.

User steering adds shallow forward searching by alternating one driven wheel, an inward heading after edge avoidance, and a watchdog for sustained motion commands without target/white observations. Missed observations imply suspicion rather than known chassis immobility. Separate failed-turn detection uses achievable gyro change.

## Diagnostic correction: blocked wheels were airborne

Offline analysis of old 12b/13 seed 11 swapped found the bot was already upside down at its 32.647 s alarm. Chassis tilt was 176.45 degrees, its origin 102.62 mm high and both tire lower surfaces approximately 19.30 mm above the mat. During the following 800 ms, encoders rotated hundreds of degrees while ground translation was under 0.017 mm and physical yaw span under 0.00011 degrees. This is loss of traction from inversion, not evidence that upright gyro turns cannot break a duel.

The roll grew from about 13.5 degrees at 12.85 s to 24 degrees at 12.90 s, 34 degrees at 12.95 s and over 90 degrees by 13.15 s. Full-duty pressure continued through that interval. These are sampled 50 ms poses and do not prove a particular contact impulse caused the flip. Pitch remained much smaller, so pitch-only protection would miss it. A tilt guard can be tested for prevention; the fixed minimal build has no demonstrated self-righting capability.

`tools/recovery_analysis.py` evaluates saved frames/events after a bout, including posture, actual ground travel, contact lag, achieved heading and sustained separation. None of this observer information reaches a controller. Older proximity labels did not establish upright posture. Prior false alerts and all failures remain preserved.

It establishes a mobile search baseline but has no enabled recovery action. The next experiment adds a three-second blind-drive watchdog and bounded pressure release/recharge, retaining the same search policy.

Loop 15 logged three blind-drive alerts across duty bouts and none against memory. All had recent actual contact, but sampled pre-alert chassis travel exceeded 0.20 m: these are missed-observation suspicions, not stationary lockup measurements. The brief release produced no new sustained separation. Stable range/yaw gates never fired. Loop 16 adds a 12-degree pitch-or-roll guard with 200 ms reverse release; beyond 75 degrees it brakes rather than spin airborne wheels. This tests prevention, with front-color priority and bounded reverse exposure. Runtime v2 metadata identifies the tilt extension; three frozen legacy digests and independent source-level review establish unchanged previous channels/mechanics.

Loop 16 won all six duty bouts (12.308–43.589 s) but drew all six memory bouts. It meets the pooled comparison bar with no faults. In duty seed 23, original sides, a guard fired at 25.026 s: sampled tilt declined from 14.635 to 0.874 degrees over the following 1.5 s, with no new sustained separation. Five other wins occurred without a guard trigger; adding a scheduled tilt read changes policy timing by 1 ms. These outcomes therefore establish a complete-program comparison, not proof that tilt protection caused its score. Loop 18 will disable only the guard while retaining identical read/scheduler cost. Loop 17 first tests a four-second close-engagement budget (80% close coverage and a fresh close return), independent of gyro span, then a 200 ms retreat and 35-degree bounded turn. Useful constant-range pushing can also trigger it.

Loop 17 caught twelve close-engagement alerts against memory, but no new sustained separation before six losses. Six post-alert windows were cut short by match resolution. The criterion detects ongoing encounters, but reversing pressure is tactically costly here. Keep this distinction: better alert coverage did not mean better match outcomes. Loop 19 will test a forward differential hook while maintaining pressure, after the timing ablation in loop 18.

Loop 18 confirms the important timing confound: five duty trajectories are exactly identical to loop 16 at every recorded bot state and end time. In seed 23 original sides, the first difference is the 25.050 s frame following the guard at 25.026 s; both ultimately win, but guard-enabled resolution is 43.589 s versus 112.400 s without it. This supports a changed trajectory/faster resolution in that paired uncalibrated case, not a proven prevention of inversion or improved win count. The complete program retains its provisional selection because the guard offers posture protection without reducing training score.

Loop 19 fired 150 alerts against memory across six full-duration draws; all were at observed close proximity, but none produced strict new sustained separation. A timed forward differential pulse therefore preserved the duel more effectively than retreat, without resolving it. Loop 20 drives one wheel forward while holding the other, ending on 35 degrees achieved in the requested direction or a 450 ms deadline. Rotation imposed by an opponent in the opposite direction cannot falsely complete that target.

Legacy loops 14–20 initialize the “since white” watchdog anchor at opening completion until the first actual white reading. Their logged age does not prove a white touch. Loop 21 and subsequent sources explicitly use unknown (-1) before a real white observation; the blind watchdog still measures sustained forward command time. A border heading is a return-along-recent-path hint, not a recovered inward normal or certified position. Wheel travel only ages that hint and cannot certify clearance under slip.

Loop 20 produced three strict new sustained separations against memory (one matching trajectory per swapped-side seed): the 81.161 s alert was followed by 0.75 s with no contact and chassis distance over 180 mm, 42.26 degrees achieved yaw and 197 mm endpoint separation growth. Both bots stayed upright. Two of these bouts nevertheless ended in losses at 113.879 s and one drew. An observed escape is not sufficient evidence of a superior strategy. Loop 21 uses elapsed time and cumulative absolute wheel travel since a real white touch to age a recent return-path heading hint; otherwise it alternates hook direction.

Loop 21 improves the complete-program training score to 83.3% and is loaded as `weave_hook` in the UI against close-memory, with its exact six-win source and winning replay. Independent audit finds **zero border-bias invocations**: all 86 direction choices use alternating fallback, despite 45 white logs against duty and none against memory. No tilt guards fire. There are no strict new sustained separations; the memory wins occur while the bots stay upright and engaged. Direction logging adds a scheduled 1 ms call compared with loop 20. Treat this as a whole-program result with timing sensitivity, not evidence for geometric direction selection or a causal escape improvement. Loop 22 tests legal full-duty pressure with the same bounded gyro hook.

Loop 22 has no losses but eight draws in twelve bouts. It does not beat loop 21's pooled score. None of its 226 alerts produces strict new sustained separation. The final loop 23 keeps loop 21's call/log schedule and changes close-engagement direction: follow a significant recent unwrapped gyro trend, because the earlier opposing hooks repeatedly failed their requested yaw while aligned hooks nearly attained their targets. This is contact steering from onboard rotation, not an opponent-bearing estimate. Blind-drive recovery retains alternating direction.

All ten candidates completed twelve bouts each, with no program/rule faults. Loop 23 ties loop 21's outcomes against each fixed opponent. Against memory it resolves at 45.930–96.389 s versus loop 21's 32.435–43.405 s, so the tie does not justify replacing the selected program. Sources 21 and 23 are frozen for fresh-seed comparison and direct challenge; no tuning follows those results.

## Heading contract correction

A signed-frame audit after these trials found the existing heading RPC counterclockwise-positive, contrary to Pybricks' clockwise-positive contract. A physical +30-degree world-Y rotation points the +Z nose toward +X (left), so Pybricks must report -30 degrees. The correction belongs at the runtime RPC boundary, with no changes to World mechanics, raw observer yaw or sensor RNG. New `21b_clockwise_compatible.py` and `23b_clockwise_compatible.py` explicitly convert real clockwise heading to the established internal CCW coordinates; their policy, hardware-call count and timing are unchanged. Original v1/v2 sources and metadata remain immutable. Runtime v3 records the contract correction; the same 24 frozen bouts were repeated as compatibility verification, comparing exact command/events/state/outcome evidence before applying the score to the revised live source. This is a compatibility repair, not a new tuned strategy or a second independent holdout sample.

Compatibility verification passes: **all 24 v2/v3 pairs have exactly identical complete saved frames and events**, including motor commands, operation counts, sensor/pose snapshots and outcomes. Independent review reproduced this comparison. They are repeated equivalence checks, not extra evidence of strategy generalization. Across the project, 314 recorded bouts comprise 290 distinct trials and 24 compatibility repeats, all without program/rule faults. `experiments/results.json` preserves compact outcomes and event evidence; `experiments/heading-compatibility.json` records the repair check.

The UI now runs v3 with corrected live feedback presets and selects exact revision 21b against close-memory. Its displayed **6W/0D/2L** is the fresh eight-bout opponent score; pooled fresh results are **9W/3D/4L (65.625%)**. Inspect winning bout loads the corrected-runtime seed-607 original-side victory at 43.405 s. `rotation_steering` exposes the final challenger and `duty_hook` the loss-free but slower full-duty alternative. Original archived sources remain tied to their captured v1/v2 profiles; a historical replay is valid for inspection but its signed reads require conversion before a new v3 run.

The strongest findings are diagnostic: old stalled turns were on an inverted chassis; later duels rotate while moving, defeating low-yaw detection; backing can concede ground without separating; achieved separation can still lose; and 1 ms call timing materially affects trajectories. The simulator remains uncalibrated. Before treating any ranking as hardware guidance, measure mounting/gyro error, contact/slip and real Pybricks call/print timing. Broader calibration belongs in the roadmap, without changing this round's mechanics to favor a policy.
